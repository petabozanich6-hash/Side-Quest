## Child mission
A museum printed 312 tickets but only 268 were used. How many were unused? Learn to find a difference by moving **up** from the smaller number rather than repeatedly guessing or borrowing digits.

**Success:** I can state what a difference means, use friendly jumps through hundreds, show each jump in a labelled diagram, compare with another method and verify by addition.

### Teach 1 — Subtraction compares two positions
**Rule:** The difference between 268 and 312 is the distance between those numbers. **Why:** Counting forward from the smaller to the larger tells us how much separates them.
**Model:** From 268 jump +2 to 270, +30 to 300, +12 to 312. Total 2 + 30 + 12 = **44**, so 312 − 268 = 44.
**Misconception:** Answer 56 because 312 − 200 = 112 then guess the remaining subtraction. **Fix:** Record all parts of one continuous path, not disconnected guesses.
**Small try:** From 289 to 320: +11 to 300 and +20 to 320, so difference = **31**.
**Check:** Why is jumping from 289 to 310 by +11 wrong? Because 289 + 11 = 300, not 310.

### Teach 2 — Use friendly tens or hundreds as stepping stones
**Rule:** Break a large gap into easy chunks. **Why:** Hundreds are reliable checkpoints that reduce mental load.
**Model:** 405 − 376: start at 376. +4 to 380, +20 to 400, +5 to 405. Add 4 + 20 + 5 = **29**.
**Misconception:** Using 380 → 400 as +200 because the hundreds digit changes. **Fix:** Subtract the endpoints: 400 − 380 = 20.
**Small try:** 502 − 478: +2 to 480, +20 to 500, +2 to 502; answer **24**.
**Check:** The jumps themselves are addition, but what is the subtraction answer? Their total, 24.

### Teach 3 — Check by reversing the operation
**Rule:** If 312 − 268 = 44, then 268 + 44 must equal 312. **Why:** Addition recombines the removed part and remaining amount.
**Model:** 268 + 40 = 308, +4 = 312. This confirms the difference 44.
**Misconception:** Checking by subtracting 44 from 268. **Fix:** Start from the smaller number and add the alleged difference.
**Small try:** Verify 600 − 547 = 53: 547 + 53 = 600.
**Check:** If 547 + 63 = 610, why can't 63 be the difference to 600?

### Teach 4 — Choose an efficient subtraction strategy
**Rule:** Counting up is especially handy when the numbers are close; subtracting in parts can also work. **Why:** Flexibility avoids unnecessarily complicated regrouping.
**Model:** 703 − 695: start at 695; +5 to 700, +3 to 703; difference **8**. Doing hundreds-column borrowing for such a small gap would be harder.
**Misconception:** Assuming a large starting number demands a large answer. **Fix:** Compare how close the two endpoints actually are.
**Small try:** 1001 − 996: +4 to 1000, +1 to 1001; difference **5**.
**Check:** A gap of 5 is reasonable even though both numbers have four digits.

## Worked example
A shop had 425 reusable bags on Monday and 387 on Friday. How many fewer? 387 → 390 (+3) → 400 (+10) → 425 (+25). Total **38**. Check 387 + 38 = 425.

## Guided practice
1. **521 − 486:** +4 to 490, +10 to 500, +21 to 521. Total **35**. If you obtained 25, inspect whether you omitted the jump from 490 to 500.
2. **804 − 796:** +4 to 800 and +4 to 804. Difference **8**. If your answer is 108, ask how close the numbers are.

## Independent evidence
1. Find **632 − 587** using a labelled count-up diagram.
2. Find **903 − 876** using a bridge through 900.
3. Calculate **715 − 698** two ways; verify with addition.
4. There are **431** science kits, of which **396** have been borrowed. How many remain? Explain why the answer is plausible.

## Eight multiple-choice checks
1. 300 − 296 = ? A 104; **B 4**; C 14; D 6. *Feedback:* 296 + 4 = 300.
2. From 478 to 480 is? A +12; **B +2**; C +22; D +20. *Feedback:* 480 − 478 equals 2.
3. The gap 380 → 400 is? A 200; **B 20**; C 120; D 2. *Feedback:* Four hundred is twenty more than three hundred eighty.
4. 405 − 376 = ? **A 29**; B 31; C 39; D 119. *Feedback:* +4, +20 and +5 make 29.
5. Check 501 − 484 = 17 using? A 501 + 17 = 484; **B 484 + 17 = 501**; C 484 − 17 = 501; D 501 − 484 = 27. *Feedback:* Add the difference to the smaller number.
6. 700 − 699 = ? A 101; B 11; **C 1**; D 0. *Feedback:* Consecutive numbers have a difference of one.
7. Which count-up for 532 − 498 works? **A +2 to 500, +32 to 532**; B +2 to 500, +30 to 532; C +12 to 500, +32 to 532; D +34 to 500. *Feedback:* The jumps must land on their labelled endpoints.
8. 811 − 788 = ? A 13; **B 23**; C 33; D 123. *Feedback:* +12 reaches 800, then +11 reaches 811, giving 23.

**Written A:** A learner says 602 − 589 = 113 because the first number is much larger. Correct the error with a labelled path and a check.
**Written B:** Explain why 1003 − 998 has a small answer without doing column subtraction.

## Three-stage hints for each screen
- **Mission:** Find the unused amount → think of a difference → start at the smaller ticket number and count up.
- **Teach 1:** Think distance → list checkpoints → 268→270→300→312.
- **Teach 2:** Aim for round numbers → label each actual distance → 376→380 is 4, not 40.
- **Teach 3:** Turn subtraction into an addition check → add the difference to the smaller number → 268 + 44 should reach 312.
- **Teach 4:** Look at the relative positions → choose few convenient jumps → for 703 and 695 the gap is just 5 + 3.
- **Worked:** Count three jumps → add them only once → 3 + 10 + 25 = 38.
- **Guided:** Show 500 as a checkpoint → split the journey at 490 then 500 → 486→490→500→521.
- **Independent:** Label start and target → select a tens/hundreds checkpoint → total all gaps and check by addition.
- **Quiz:** Imagine the line → eliminate values bigger than the apparent gap → 700 and 699 differ by only one.
- **Submit:** Keep each diagram → include one addition check → write which two numbers you compared.

## Original two-character episode
**Pip:** “One number has three digits, so the difference must be huge.”
**Juno:** “What about 700 and 699? They're neighbours.”
**Pip:** “Oh. Their difference is just one!”
**Juno:** “It's the distance, not the number of digits, that matters.”
**Pause:** Find your own pair of three-digit numbers only two apart.

## Parent-only key
1. 632 − 587 = **45**: +3 to 590, +10 to 600, +32 to 632.
2. 903 − 876 = **27**: +4 to 880, +20 to 900, +3 to 903.
3. 715 − 698 = **17**: +2 to 700, +15 to 715; verify 698 + 17 = 715.
4. 431 − 396 = **35**: +4 to 400, +31 to 431; 396 + 35 = 431.
Written A: 589 + 11 = 600, +2 = 602, so difference is 13, not 113; addition check 589 + 13 = 602. Written B: 998 + 2 = 1000, +3 = 1003; difference 5.
Assess clear checkpoints, correct jump sizes and independent checks, not just answers. **Extension:** Invent two different count-up paths for the same subtraction and prove both have equal total. **Screen-free:** Use three numbered paper cards as checkpoints and write arrow labels between them.
