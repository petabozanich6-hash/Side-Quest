# Number sense warm-ups — Year 6, Week 1, Lesson 4
**Lesson ID:** s3-mat-y6-w01-l4 · **Subject:** Mathematics · **Unit:** Large numbers and estimation · **Role:** Fluency and review · **Duration:** 55–60 minutes. Preserve any existing attached outcomes in the app.

## Your mission
Number sense means recognising how numbers are built, how large they are, and whether an answer is reasonable. Today is not a race. You will retrieve what you learned about reading large numbers, place value, expanded form and ordering, then use those ideas to explain decisions. You may use a place-value chart if you need one.

**Success:** Read a large number, name a digit's value, expand and rebuild a number, compare close values, and check your own work.

## 1. Retrieval without notes (7 minutes)
Write your first answer, then reveal the feedback. Do not worry about a mistake; use it to decide what to revisit.

**R1.** Write 8,040,306 in words. **Answer:** Eight million, forty thousand, three hundred and six. **Feedback:** Group digits in threes from the right: 8 | 040 | 306. Read each nonzero group with its unit.
**R2.** In 7,205,091, what is the value of 2? **Answer:** 200,000. **Feedback:** The 2 is in hundred-thousands, not ten-thousands.
**R3.** Expand 3,060,402. **Answer:** 3,000,000 + 60,000 + 400 + 2. **Feedback:** Zero placeholders stay in the numeral; zero-value terms may be omitted from the sum.
**R4.** Rebuild 5,000,000 + 90,000 + 7,000 + 80 + 1. **Answer:** 5,097,081. **Feedback:** Fill every column from millions to ones.
**R5.** Which is larger, 6,405,900 or 6,450,090? **Answer:** 6,450,090. **Feedback:** At ten-thousands, 5 beats 0 after the first two places tie.
**R6.** True or false: 10,000,000 < 9,999,999. **Answer:** False. **Feedback:** Eight digits make the first number larger than any seven-digit positive whole number.

**Teacher note:** Record which types of errors occur. The next station addresses those exact misconceptions.

## 2. Mini-lesson: flexible ways to see a number (8 minutes)
The same number can be represented in different ways without changing its value.

**4,520,000** is:
- four million, five hundred and twenty thousand;
- 4,000,000 + 500,000 + 20,000;
- 4 million + 52 ten-thousands;
- 452 groups of ten thousand.

**Why these agree:** 52 × 10,000 = 520,000, and 452 × 10,000 = 4,520,000. Grouping differently is allowed as long as the total remains unchanged. Standard expanded form usually names each nonzero place separately; flexible regrouping helps with mental calculation.

**Worked check:** Is 3,400,000 equal to 34 × 100,000? Yes: 34 hundred-thousands make 3,400,000.

**Misconception:** Writing 3,400,000 = 3 + 4 is wrong: the digits must carry their place values.

**Try:** Complete 6,300,000 = ___ × 100,000. **Answer:** 63. **Hint:** How many hundred-thousands make six million three hundred thousand?

## 3. Four challenge stations (20 minutes)
Work through each station. Answers follow each station; hide them until your attempt.

### Station A — Missing place
A1. 2,0_5,700 is 2,045,700. What digit fills the blank? **4.**
A2. 9,300,000 + 60,000 + 400 + 8 = ? **9,360,408.**
A3. 7,0_0,090 has a 5 in the hundred-thousands place. Write the full number. **7,500,090.**
**Explanation:** Position is determined by the column, not by how many zeros you happen to see.

### Station B — Which is closest?
B1. Which is closer to 5,000,000: 4,990,000 or 5,020,000? **4,990,000**, which is 10,000 away; the other is 20,000 away.
B2. Which is closer to 8,500,000: 8,490,000 or 8,520,000? **8,490,000**, 10,000 away versus 20,000.
B3. Explain how you checked. **Model:** Compare each distance from the target, not simply which number looks larger.
**Important:** A larger number is not automatically the closest.

### Station C — Spot the false claim
C1. “7,040,020 has four hundred-thousands.” **False.** It has zero hundred-thousands and four ten-thousands.
C2. “6,000,000 + 70,000 + 5 = 6,070,005.” **True.**
C3. “3,009,100 > 3,090,010.” **False.** At ten-thousands, 0 < 9.
C4. “A zero can be left out of standard form without changing the number.” **False in general.** Internal zeros hold place; 5,040 ≠ 540.

### Station D — Make your own number
Use each digit 0, 1, 3, 6, 8, 9 exactly once.
D1. Make the greatest six-digit number. **986,310.**
D2. Make the smallest six-digit number. **103,689** (a six-digit numeral cannot begin with zero).
D3. Find a number between 600,000 and 700,000. **Example:** 631,890. Accept any valid arrangement beginning with 6.
D4. Explain why the greatest number begins with 9 and the smallest begins with 1. **Model:** The leftmost position has the greatest place value; a leading zero would not make a six-digit number.

## 4. Reasoning investigation — the treasure ledger (10 minutes)
Three imaginary treasure stores report:
- Hill Cave: 8,406,250 tokens
- River Vault: 8,460,205 tokens
- Forest Cache: 8,406,520 tokens

**Q1:** Order from smallest to largest. **Answer:** Hill Cave; Forest Cache; River Vault.
**Q2:** How many more tokens are in Forest Cache than Hill Cave? **Answer:** 270. Work: 8,406,520 − 8,406,250 = 270.
**Q3:** Is River Vault closer to 8,400,000 or 8,500,000? **Answer:** 8,500,000; distances 60,205 and 39,795 respectively.
**Q4:** A sign says “All three stores contain more than 8.45 million tokens.” Is this true? **Answer:** No. Only River Vault exceeds 8,450,000.
**Q5:** Write a two-sentence report using one comparison and one difference. **Model:** “River Vault has the most tokens at 8,460,205. Forest Cache has 270 more tokens than Hill Cave.”

**Rubric:** 1 point each Q1–Q4; Q5 2 points (accurate comparison and accurate difference). Total 6. If Q3 is wrong, compare distances on a number line; if Q4 is wrong, rewrite 8.45 million as 8,450,000.

## 5. Exit ticket (8 minutes)
E1. Expand 9,008,070. **Answer:** 9,000,000 + 8,000 + 70. (2 points)
E2. Write 4,000,000 + 300,000 + 90 + 2 in standard form. **Answer:** 4,300,092. (2)
E3. Order 5,050,500; 5,500,050; 5,005,500 ascending. **Answer:** 5,005,500; 5,050,500; 5,500,050. (2)
E4. A student says 6,070,000 has seven hundred-thousands. Correct them. **Model:** It has zero hundred-thousands and seven ten-thousands; the 7 is worth 70,000. (2)

**Total 8:** 7–8 confident; 5–6 review missed skill and retry; 0–4 return to the place-value chart and worked examples. These are instructional thresholds.

**Targeted retries:** For E1 expand 2,040,600 → 2,000,000 + 40,000 + 600; for E2 rebuild 7,000,000 + 50,000 + 4 → 7,050,004; for E3 sort 3,003,000; 3,030,000; 3,300,000; for E4 name the value of 8 in 9,080,000 → 80,000.
