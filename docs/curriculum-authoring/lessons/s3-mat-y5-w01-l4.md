# The number detective's training room

**Year 5 · Mathematics · Week 1 Lesson 4 · 55–60 minutes.** Keep existing attached outcomes.

## How to become fluent without rushing

A number detective needs to notice patterns, spot mistakes and decide whether an answer makes sense. Fluency is not simply answering as fast as possible. It means recognising the structure of a number so you can work accurately, then gradually becoming more efficient.

Suppose you see **5,090,004**. Instead of reading each digit separately, your eyes can recognise **5 million | 090 thousand | 004 ones**. That means five million, ninety thousand, four. You can also see that the 9 is worth 90,000. If someone says this number is smaller than 5,009,400, you can check the ten-thousands column and explain why that claim is false. These are different uses of the same understanding.

Today you'll move through four short challenges. Read the explanations carefully whenever a result surprises you.

## Challenge 1 — read and rebuild

Imagine an imaginary stadium reporting **7,204,016** visitors across many years. Say the number aloud before continuing. It is **seven million, two hundred and four thousand, sixteen**. The group 016 means sixteen, not one hundred and six.

Now write the expanded form: **7,000,000 + 200,000 + 4,000 + 10 + 6**. The zeros still matter in the numeral even though we omit zero terms from the addition.

A second report says **3,000,000 + 50,000 + 600 + 9**. Put the amounts in their columns. The result is **3,050,609**. If you wrote 3,50,609, remember that the thousands group must have three positions.

**Try independently:** Read 8,040,306. **Eight million, forty thousand, three hundred and six.** Expand 2,006,080. **2,000,000 + 6,000 + 80.** Rebuild 4,000,000 + 90,000 + 5,000 + 70 + 2. **4,095,072.**

## Challenge 2 — zeros are not decoration

A student writes **6,040,200 = 6,000,000 + 400,000 + 200**. What went wrong? The 4 is in the ten-thousands column, not the hundred-thousands column. The correct expansion is **6,000,000 + 40,000 + 200**.

This is a useful kind of mistake because it reveals *why* the zero before the 4 matters. Without it, the 4 could move into the wrong position and become ten times larger.

**Try:** Find the error in “9,003,050 is nine million, thirty thousand, fifty.” **Answer:** 003 is three thousand, not thirty thousand. The correct reading is **nine million, three thousand, fifty**.

**Try:** Find the error in “2,080,005 has eight hundred-thousands.” **Answer:** The 8 is in ten-thousands, so it is worth **80,000**.

## Challenge 3 — decide which is closer

Imagine a number line from 5,000,000 to 6,000,000. A marker is placed at **5,490,000**. Is it closer to five million or six million? It is 490,000 above five million and 510,000 below six million, so it is **closer to five million**. It is tempting to see the 49 and think it is nearly 50, but distances provide a reliable check.

Now compare **4,990,000** and **5,020,000** as candidates closest to 5,000,000. The first is 10,000 away; the second is 20,000 away. So **4,990,000** is closer, even though it is smaller.

**Try:** Which is closer to 8,500,000: 8,490,000 or 8,520,000? **8,490,000**; it is 10,000 away, compared with 20,000. Which is closer to 3,000,000: 2,980,000 or 3,050,000? **2,980,000**, 20,000 away versus 50,000.

## Challenge 4 — construct the number

Use the digits **0, 1, 3, 6, 8, 9** exactly once to make the greatest six-digit number. Put the greatest available digit in the largest place first. The answer is **986,310**.

For the smallest six-digit number, you cannot put 0 first: that would leave only five digits in the actual number. Start with the smallest nonzero digit, **1**, then use 0 and the remaining digits in ascending order. The answer is **103,689**.

**Try:** Make a number between 600,000 and 700,000 using each digit once. **Example:** 631,890. Any valid six-digit arrangement beginning with 6 is acceptable.

## Apply: the treasure ledger

Three imaginary stores report Hill Cave **8,406,250** tokens, River Vault **8,460,205**, and Forest Cache **8,406,520**.

Arrange them from smallest to largest. **Hill Cave, Forest Cache, River Vault.** Notice how the first two tie through thousands and differ at hundreds.

How many more tokens are in Forest Cache than Hill Cave? Subtract 8,406,520 − 8,406,250 to get **270**.

Is River Vault closer to 8,400,000 or 8,500,000? Its distances are **60,205** and **39,795**, so it is closer to **8,500,000**.

A sign claims that all three contain more than 8.45 million tokens. Convert 8.45 million to **8,450,000**. Only River Vault exceeds that amount. The sign is **false**.

## Final check

**1 (2).** Expand 9,008,070. **9,000,000 + 8,000 + 70.**  
**2 (2).** Rebuild 4,000,000 + 300,000 + 90 + 2. **4,300,092.**  
**3 (2).** Order 5,050,500; 5,500,050; 5,005,500 ascending. **5,005,500; 5,050,500; 5,500,050.**  
**4 (2).** Correct “6,070,000 has seven hundred-thousands.” **It has seven ten-thousands, worth 70,000.**
