# Reading and writing numbers to the millions
**ID:** s3-mat-y5-w01-l1 · Year 5 · Stage 3 · Mathematics · Week 1, Lesson 1 · Unit: Whole numbers to the millions · Role: Concept · 55–60 minutes.

**Learning mission:** Read, say and write whole numbers into the millions in standard and word form. Identify the three-digit groups that make long numbers easier to read. Existing attached outcome metadata is to be preserved; do not invent codes.

## Opening: the museum's enormous count (5 minutes)
A museum says it has **2,405,018** tiny fossils in its imaginary collection. Try reading the number aloud. Write it in words. How can the commas help?

**Model:** “Two million, four hundred and five thousand, eighteen.” The commas divide the numeral into groups: **2 | 405 | 018**. Read each group from left to right and give the group its name.

**Pip:** “Why don't we read every zero?”
**Juno:** “A zero holds a place in the written number, but we do not usually name every zero when speaking.”

## Teach: grouping by threes (12 minutes)
A whole number is made from digits 0–9. We read large whole numbers in groups of three digits, starting from the right. The rightmost group is **ones**; the next is **thousands**; the next is **millions**.

| Millions group | Thousands group | Ones group |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 2 | 405 | 018 |

Read 2 as “two million”; 405 as “four hundred and five thousand”; 018 as “eighteen”. Join the parts.

**Why three-digit groups?** Each group contains hundreds, tens and ones. Moving left by one complete group makes the units one thousand times as large. The commas help our eyes find the groups; they are not digits.

**Worked example A: 6,032,409.** Split: 6 | 032 | 409. Say: **six million, thirty-two thousand, four hundred and nine**. Notice the 0 in 032 does not make the group “three hundred and two”; the group is 32 thousand.

**Worked example B: 9,000,705.** Split: 9 | 000 | 705. Say: **nine million, seven hundred and five**. The zero thousands group is not spoken.

**Worked example C: words to digits.** “Four million, seventy thousand, six hundred and two.”
- Millions group: 4
- Thousands group: 070 (seventy thousand)
- Ones group: 602
- Result: **4,070,602**.
**Common error:** 4,70,602 is not correct three-digit grouping. Write every middle group with three digits.

### Place-value bridge
In 5,240,013, the digit 5 means five millions; 2 means two hundred-thousands; 4 means four ten-thousands; 1 means one ten; 3 means three ones. A digit's **value** depends on its place. The next lesson will explore that idea more deeply.

## Guided practice (12 minutes)
Reveal the hint only after an attempt.

**G1.** Group and read 3,108,050. **Answer:** Three million, one hundred and eight thousand, fifty. **Hint:** 3 | 108 | 050. **Feedback:** The final group 050 means fifty, not five hundred.

**G2.** Write “seven million, twenty-four thousand, nine” in digits. **Answer:** 7,024,009. **Hint:** Millions 7; thousands 024; ones 009. **Feedback:** Each group after millions needs three digits, even if it begins with zero.

**G3.** Read 1,000,001. **Answer:** One million and one. **Feedback:** The thousands group is zero; do not say “one thousand”.

**G4.** Which numeral means “eight million, five thousand, forty”? Options: 8,500,040; 8,005,040; 8,050,040. **Answer:** 8,005,040. **Feedback:** Five thousand is 005 in the thousands group; 050 would be fifty thousand.

**G5.** Correct this: “2,060,300 is two million, six hundred thousand, three hundred.” **Answer:** Two million, **sixty thousand**, three hundred. **Feedback:** 060 thousands is 60 thousand, not 600 thousand.

**G6.** Write “nine hundred and twelve thousand, six” in digits. **Answer:** 912,006. **Hint:** There is no millions group. Thousands = 912; ones = 006.

## Independent practice (12 minutes)
Write words for I1–I4 and numerals for I5–I8. Check your work by splitting each number into groups.

I1. **4,020,106** → **Four million, twenty thousand, one hundred and six**.
I2. **8,300,009** → **Eight million, three hundred thousand, nine**.
I3. **705,040** → **Seven hundred and five thousand, forty**.
I4. **2,002,002** → **Two million, two thousand and two**.
I5. **Six million, eight thousand, thirty** → **6,008,030**.
I6. **Three million, four hundred thousand, five** → **3,400,005**.
I7. **One million, ninety thousand, seventy-one** → **1,090,071**.
I8. **Nine hundred and six thousand, four hundred and ten** → **906,410**.

**Common mistakes and targeted feedback:**
- Writing 6,8,30 for I5: “Write three digits in the thousands and ones groups: 008 and 030.”
- Reading 705,040 as seven million: “There are only two groups; the left group is thousands.”
- Dropping zeros: “The zero may be silent when spoken but necessary when written.”

## Apply: write the science headlines (8 minutes)
A fictional space museum has these counts:
- Star pictures: 1,205,030
- Planet pictures: 980,006
- Comet pictures: 3,040,080

**A.** Write each count in words.  
**Answers:** One million, two hundred and five thousand, thirty; nine hundred and eighty thousand, six; three million, forty thousand, eighty.

**B.** A caption reads “three million, four hundred thousand and eighty” for comet pictures. Is it correct?  
**Answer:** No. That would be 3,400,080. The correct count is 3,040,080: **forty thousand**, not four hundred thousand.

**C.** Create a new imaginary count with a zero in the thousands group and explain how it is read.  
**Example:** 2,000,415 — two million, four hundred and fifteen. Accept any correct numeral and reading with a 000 thousands group.

**Rubric (6):** 3 for accurate A, 2 for B with correction, 1 for a valid C.

## Exit ticket (7 minutes)
E1 (2): Write **5,070,009** in words. **Five million, seventy thousand, nine.**
E2 (2): Write **“two million, six thousand, fourteen”** in digits. **2,006,014.**
E3 (2): Explain why **4,005,080** does not mean “four million, fifty thousand, eighty.” **Model:** The thousands group is 005, which is five thousand, not fifty thousand; the correct reading is four million, five thousand, eighty.
E4 (2): Read **900,090** in words. **Nine hundred thousand, ninety.**

**Score guidance:** 7–8 proceed; 5–6 practise the missed group; 0–4 rebuild groups using a chart. **Retry examples:** 3,004,020 → three million, four thousand, twenty; “five million, twelve thousand, three” → 5,012,003.
