# The museum with a million tiny treasures

Imagine a museum that collects tiny objects: ancient beads, polished stones and miniature fossils. A new shipment has arrived, and the curator has written a number on the crate:

## 2,405,018

The curator needs to telephone another museum and say exactly how many objects arrived. She cannot simply point at the digits. She has to turn that long numeral into words that someone else can understand.

How would you say it?

You might recognise the **2** at the beginning and the **18** at the end. But what does the **405** in the middle mean? Why are there commas? And why doesn't the curator say every zero?

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to read numbers like this confidently and write them down when someone says them aloud. You will learn a method that works even when a number has several zeros, because those are often the numbers that trick us.

## Part 1 — What the commas are telling you

You already know how to read a three-digit number. For example, **405** is *four hundred and five*. The 4 tells us there are four hundreds, the 0 tells us there are no tens, and the 5 tells us there are five ones.

A long number is easier to understand when we split it into **groups of three digits**, starting at the right-hand end.

Look again at the museum's number:

**2 | 405 | 018**

The three groups have different jobs.

| Millions | Thousands | Ones |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| **2** | **405** | **018** |

The group on the far right tells us how many **ones** there are. The group in the middle tells us how many **thousands** there are. The group on the left tells us how many **millions** there are.

We read the groups **from left to right**, saying the name of the group as we go.

Start with the left group: **2 million**.

Move to the middle group: **405 thousand**, which we say as **four hundred and five thousand**.

Finish with the right group: **018**. Although it is written using three positions in the chart, its value is just **eighteen**. The zero at the front does not make it “zero hundred and eighteen”.

Put those parts together:

**2,405,018 = two million, four hundred and five thousand, eighteen.**

Notice something important: we did **not** read the whole string digit by digit. Saying “two, four, zero, five, zero, one, eight” would describe the symbols, but it would not tell the curator the number of objects in the usual way.

### Why does each group have three places?

Within a group, the positions are **hundreds, tens and ones**. After three places, we move into the next group of a thousand.

One thousand is 1,000 ones. One million is 1,000 thousands.

That is why the groups have names. The same familiar three-digit reading skill can be reused again and again. You read the digits in a group, then add **thousand** or **million** when that group needs a name.

A comma is a **separator**, not a digit. It helps you see where one group ends and the next begins. You do not say “comma” when reading the number aloud.

**Illustration A — required:** Show `2,405,018` above three aligned labelled blocks: `2` (millions), `405` (thousands), `018` (ones). Under each block show its spoken contribution: “two million”, “four hundred and five thousand”, “eighteen”. The diagram must be mathematically accurate and legible on a small screen. **Alt text:** “The number 2,405,018 is divided into three groups: 2 millions, 405 thousands and 018 ones. It is read two million, four hundred and five thousand, eighteen.”

### Try this with me

Read **3,216,407**. First, cover the last two groups with your hand or imagine looking at only the first group. You see **3**. That is **three million**.

Now look at **216**. You already know how to say that: **two hundred and sixteen**. Because it is in the thousands group, it becomes **two hundred and sixteen thousand**.

Finally, read **407**: **four hundred and seven**.

The complete number is **three million, two hundred and sixteen thousand, four hundred and seven**.

**Check question:** In **3,216,407**, does the middle group mean two hundred and sixteen, or two hundred and sixteen **thousand**?

**Answer:** Two hundred and sixteen thousand. Its position gives the group its name.

If you said only “two hundred and sixteen”, you read the digits correctly but forgot the group's value. The correction is simple: look at the label above the group before you say it.

## Part 2 — Zeros have a job, even when we do not say them

Some large numbers look especially confusing because they contain zeros. Consider:

## 6,032,409

Split the number into groups:

**6 | 032 | 409**

The millions group is **6**, so we begin with **six million**.

The thousands group is **032**. You may be tempted to say “zero hundred and thirty-two thousand”. We do not need to do that. The zero tells us there are no hundreds *within that group*. The group represents **32 thousand**, so we say **thirty-two thousand**.

The ones group is **409**, which is **four hundred and nine**.

So the full reading is:

**Six million, thirty-two thousand, four hundred and nine.**

The zeros have not disappeared from the *written* number. They are still needed to keep each digit in the correct place. But when speaking, we usually say the value of each group rather than naming its empty positions.

Now consider a different number:

## 9,000,705

This time the middle group is **000**. There are **no thousands at all**. We do not say “zero thousand”. We skip that group when reading aloud.

We say:

**Nine million, seven hundred and five.**

This is a powerful rule: **if an entire group is 000, you do not need to say that group aloud.**

But you **must** keep its three zeros when writing the numeral. Without them, **9,000,705** could be mistaken for **9,705**, which is only nine thousand, seven hundred and five. The difference is enormous.

### Compare these two numbers carefully

**4,005,080** means **four million, five thousand, eighty**.

**4,050,080** means **four million, fifty thousand, eighty**.

The digits look similar, but the middle groups are different: **005** and **050**. In the first number, the 5 is in the thousands position. In the second, it is in the ten-thousands position. That is why one number has five thousand and the other has fifty thousand.

**Illustration B — required:** Two horizontal number-group diagrams labelled `4 | 005 | 080` and `4 | 050 | 080`. Highlight the position of the 5 within the thousands group in each number. Label the contributions “5 thousand” and “50 thousand”. **Alt text:** “4,005,080 contains five thousand, while 4,050,080 contains fifty thousand because the digit 5 is in a different position.”

### Pause and answer

Read **7,004,020** aloud.

Do not rush. Find the millions group, then the thousands group, then the ones group.

**Answer:** **Seven million, four thousand, twenty.**

**If you said “seven million, forty thousand, twenty”:** look at the middle group. It is **004**, not **040**. Four in the rightmost position of the thousands group means **four thousand**.

**If you said “seven million, four thousand, two hundred”:** look at the final group. **020** is twenty. **200** would be two hundred.

## Part 3 — Turning spoken numbers back into digits

Reading a number is one direction of the skill. Writing a numeral when someone says it aloud is the reverse direction.

Suppose the museum curator says:

> “Four million, seventy thousand, six hundred and two.”

How do you write that correctly?

The safest method is to make three spaces for the groups:

| Millions | Thousands | Ones |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| ? | ??? | ??? |

First, listen for **million**. The curator said **four million**, so put **4** in the millions group.

Next, listen for **thousand**. She said **seventy thousand**. Seventy is written **70**, but the thousands group needs **three positions** because it sits between the millions and ones groups. Write it as **070**.

Finally, listen for the part after “thousand”: **six hundred and two**. That is **602**.

Now join the groups with commas:

**4 | 070 | 602 → 4,070,602.**

Read it back to check: four million, seventy thousand, six hundred and two. It matches exactly.

### Why do we write 070 instead of just 70?

Imagine writing the groups without preserving their positions: **4 | 70 | 602**. When those digits are pushed together, you risk producing a numeral that has the wrong number of places.

The middle and right groups must each have **three digits** when there is a group to their left. Writing 070 makes the seventy stay in the correct part of the thousands group.

### Worked example — a number with almost no spoken parts

Write **“two million and nine”** as a numeral.

There are two million, **no thousands**, and nine ones.

| Millions | Thousands | Ones |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| 2 | 000 | 009 |

So the number is **2,000,009**.

It is **not** 2,009. That would mean two thousand and nine, because it has no millions group.

### Worked example — a number without millions

Write **“nine hundred and twelve thousand, six”**.

There is no millions group. The thousands group is **912**. The ones group is **006** because the spoken final amount is six.

The numeral is **912,006**.

We do not need to add a leading `000,` millions group. We simply start at the largest nonzero group.

### Try it before revealing the answer

Write **“five million, twelve thousand, three”**.

Think in groups: five million | twelve thousand | three ones.

**Answer: 5,012,003.**

If you wrote **5,12,3**, the values are in the right order, but the group positions have not been preserved. Write **012** for the thousands group and **003** for the ones group.

## Part 4 — Guided practice: let the groups do the work

These questions begin with a little support and then ask you to do more of the thinking yourself. For each one, enter your answer before opening the explanation.

### Guided question 1: read the groups

**Number:** 8,104,030.

The groups are **8 | 104 | 030**.

What do we say for the middle group?

**Answer:** **One hundred and four thousand.**

Now say the entire number.

**Full answer:** **Eight million, one hundred and four thousand, thirty.**

**If you said “three hundred” at the end:** the final group is 030, not 300. A 3 in the tens position means thirty.

### Guided question 2: fill the missing group

A number is read as **“three million, forty thousand, seven.”**

| Millions | Thousands | Ones |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| 3 | ___ | 007 |

What belongs in the thousands group?

**Answer:** **040**. The complete number is **3,040,007**.

**Hint:** Forty thousand means forty groups of one thousand, so the thousands group must read “forty”.

**If you wrote 400:** that would mean four hundred thousand, ten times too much.

### Guided question 3: explain the difference

Which numeral means **“six million, five thousand, ninety”**?

A. 6,050,090  
B. 6,005,090  
C. 6,500,090

**Answer: B — 6,005,090.**

In B, the middle group is 005, which means five thousand. In A it is 050, or fifty thousand. In C it is 500, or five hundred thousand. The final group 090 means ninety in all three numbers.

### Guided question 4: repair a museum label

A label says:

**1,060,300 — “one million, six hundred thousand, three hundred.”**

Is the label accurate?

**Answer:** No. The middle group is **060**, so it means **sixty thousand**, not six hundred thousand.

The corrected reading is **one million, sixty thousand, three hundred**.

To check, compare the two possible thousands groups: **060** is sixty; **600** is six hundred.

### Guided question 5: write without a model

Write **“seven million, two hundred and three thousand, forty-one”**.

**Answer:** **7,203,041.**

The millions group is 7, the thousands group is 203, and the ones group is 041. The zero in 041 is needed because forty-one has no hundreds.

## Part 6 — Application: save the museum's exhibition labels

The museum is preparing three displays. The counts below are fictional, but the numerals must be accurate.

**Display A:** 1,205,030 miniature shells  
**Display B:** 980,006 polished stones  
**Display C:** 3,040,080 ancient beads

The curator needs printed labels that visitors can read aloud.

### Task A — write the exhibition labels

Write each count in words, using the correct group names.

**Model answers:**

- **A:** One million, two hundred and five thousand, thirty.
- **B:** Nine hundred and eighty thousand, six.
- **C:** Three million, forty thousand, eighty.

### Task B — catch a costly error

A draft label for Display C reads:

> “Three million, four hundred thousand, eighty.”

The numeral printed beside it is **3,040,080**.

Explain exactly why the words and numeral do not match.

**Model answer:** The numeral's thousands group is **040**, meaning **forty thousand**. “Four hundred thousand” would require **400** in the thousands group and would make the numeral **3,400,080**. The draft label is wrong because it gives the digit 4 the wrong place value.

**Feedback if the student only writes “the zero is wrong”:** Ask them to name the two different values—40,000 and 400,000—and show which group matches each value.

### Task C — a telephone message

Another museum calls and says:

> “Please reserve space for two million, three thousand and sixteen specimens.”

Write the number as digits, then explain how you protected the empty positions.

**Model answer:** **2,003,016**. The groups are 2 millions, 003 thousands and 016 ones. The leading zeros in the smaller groups hold the 3 in the thousands position and the 16 in the ones group.

### Task D — make a matching pair

Create your own seven-digit number that contains at least **two zero digits**, and write its name in words.

**Example:** **5,006,207 — five million, six thousand, two hundred and seven.**

**Marking guide:** Accept any seven-digit whole number whose word form matches its groups and that contains at least two zero digits. The child must supply both a numeral and its matching word form. A response with only a numeral is incomplete.

## Part 7 — Check what you can do independently

This is the final check. Complete it without hints. You can review explanations afterwards, and you may retry any missed question.

**Question 1 (2 points).** Write **5,070,009** in words.

**Question 2 (2 points).** Write **“two million, six thousand, fourteen”** in digits.

**Question 3 (2 points).** A student says **4,005,080** means “four million, fifty thousand, eighty”. Explain the mistake and give the correct reading.

**Question 4 (2 points).** Write **900,090** in words.

**Question 5 (2 points).** The words **“seven million, forty thousand, six”** are written as **7,400,006**. Is that correct? If not, write the correct numeral and explain why.

### Answer key and marking

**1.** **Five million, seventy thousand, nine.** Award 2 points for all groups correct; 1 if only one group is misread. The middle group 070 is seventy thousand.

**2.** **2,006,014.** Award 2 for the correct numeral; 1 if the group values are identified correctly but a zero placeholder is misplaced.

**3.** **Four million, five thousand, eighty.** The middle group 005 means five thousand, not fifty thousand. Award 1 for the corrected reading and 1 for explaining the difference between 005 and 050.

**4.** **Nine hundred thousand, ninety.** Award 2 for correct reading; 1 for correctly identifying the thousands but misreading 090.

**5.** **Not correct.** The correct numeral is **7,040,006**. The spoken “forty thousand” belongs in the middle group as 040, not 400. Award 1 for the correct numeral and 1 for the explanation.

**Total: 10 points.** A score of 9–10 suggests readiness to move on. A score of 7–8 suggests a short targeted retry. A score below 7 suggests working through the grouped-number examples again with support. These are practical teaching thresholds, not standardised assessment scores.

### Targeted second chances

If you struggled **reading** numbers, try **3,004,020**. The answer is **three million, four thousand, twenty**. Check each group against its label.

If you struggled **writing** numbers, try **“five million, twelve thousand, three”**. The answer is **5,012,003**. Write 5 | 012 | 003 before joining the groups.

If you struggled with **zero placeholders**, compare **6,005,040** and **6,050,040**. The first is **six million, five thousand, forty**; the second is **six million, fifty thousand, forty**. The different positions of the 5 change its value.
