# Which number is really bigger?

**Year 5 · Mathematics · Week 1 Lesson 3 · 60 minutes.** Keep existing attached outcomes.

## Two enormous collections

The imaginary Ocean Museum has **4,305,090** shells. The Mountain Museum has **4,350,009** stones. Both numbers begin with four million. Both contain the digits 3 and 5. It is tempting to look for the largest digit anywhere in each number, but that does not work. A digit's position matters more than its appearance.

To compare numbers, begin at the **largest place value**, on the left. The first digit in both numbers is 4, meaning four million, so neither is ahead yet. The next digits are both 3, meaning three hundred thousand. Still equal. At the ten-thousands column, however, Ocean has **0** and Mountain has **5**. Mountain has fifty thousand more in that column. No combination of digits in smaller columns can reverse the result, because all those smaller positions together are worth less than one full unit of the next larger position when comparing equal-length whole numbers at their first difference.

We can write **4,305,090 < 4,350,009**. The symbol **<** means *is less than*. Turn the statement around and we get **4,350,009 > 4,305,090**, where **>** means *is greater than*. The open side faces the larger number, but it is safer to read the sentence aloud than to rely on remembering the symbol's shape.

## Why we compare from the left

Think about comparing two amounts of money: one person has six million dollars, another has five million dollars. Even if the second person has many thousands and hundreds as well, five million plus all the smaller places cannot reach six million when each smaller digit is at most 9. This is why the **first different digit from the left** settles the comparison.

Watch this method with **7,208,600** and **7,280,060**. Both have 7 millions and 2 hundred-thousands. In the ten-thousands place, the first has 0 and the second has 8. Therefore **7,208,600 < 7,280,060**. We do not need to compare the hundreds or tens.

Now compare **3,405,800** and **3,405,080**. The numbers match through the thousands column. At hundreds, the first has 8 and the second has 0. So **3,405,800 > 3,405,080**. Sometimes you must look quite far right before finding the deciding place.

If the two numerals are identical, we use **=**, meaning equal. We can also compare a numeral with expanded form: **2,060,004 = 2,000,000 + 60,000 + 4**. Different writing does not necessarily mean different value.

## Ordering more than two numbers

Imagine four libraries reporting visitor totals: North **2,405,600**, East **2,450,060**, West **2,405,060** and South **2,540,006**. We need them in **ascending order**, from smallest to largest.

All four numbers have two million. South has five hundred-thousands, while the others have four, so South is the largest. Among the others, East has five ten-thousands, while North and West have zero, so East comes after those two. Finally, North and West match until the hundreds column: North has 6 hundreds and West has 0. That makes West smaller.

The ascending order is **West, North, East, South**. In numerals: **2,405,060; 2,405,600; 2,450,060; 2,540,006**.

**Descending order** means the reverse: largest to smallest. The descending list is South, East, North, West. A reliable check is to compare each neighbouring pair. In an ascending list, every number must be no smaller than the one before it.

## Practise with support

**1.** Which is greater: 9,010,005 or 9,001,500? The millions match, and the hundred-thousands match. At ten-thousands, 1 is greater than 0. **Answer: 9,010,005 > 9,001,500.**

**2.** Compare 4,672,105 and 4,671,999. The first difference is at **thousands**: 2 thousands versus 1 thousand. **Answer: 4,672,105 > 4,671,999.**

**3.** Is 10,000,000 greater than 9,999,999? Yes. The first number has eight digits and the second has seven. A positive whole number with more digits is larger, provided we are not counting leading zeros.

**4.** Put 6,070,500; 6,007,500; 6,700,050 in ascending order. Compare from the left, not from the final three digits. **Answer: 6,007,500; 6,070,500; 6,700,050.**

**5.** A student says 5,809,004 < 5,790,999 because “790,999 looks bigger than 809,004”. Explain the mistake. **Model:** Both have five million. At hundred-thousands, 8 is greater than 7, so **5,809,004 > 5,790,999**. The comparison must start with the greatest place.

## Real-world decision: the science archive

Four imaginary observatories processed these signals: Aurora **14,205,090**; Beacon **14,250,009**; Comet **14,205,900**; Delta **14,025,990**.

First, order them from fewest to most. **Answer: Delta, Aurora, Comet, Beacon.** Next, decide whether Beacon processed more than fifteen million. **No:** 14,250,009 is less than 15,000,000. Finally, find which two counts are closest. **Aurora and Comet**, separated by **810** signals. Subtracting 14,205,900 − 14,205,090 checks this.
