# Independent lesson template

Every lesson is written so the child works alone on their own computer. The parent sees outcomes, scores and evidence, and answers 'I'm stuck' flags. Holiday themed lessons are a separate section built later; core lessons here do not depend on it.

## Quest titles

Every lesson has a quest title the child sees, plus a plain subject title the parent sees.

- Child sees: 'Quest: The Compass of Commas'
- Parent sees: 'Stage 2 | Week 3 | Day 2 | Lesson 3 | English | Commas in lists'

Title rules: each quest title is short, names the real topic so it is searchable, and fits a running story for the week (for example, Week 3 is an expedition and each subject's quest is one stop on the map). Quest titles follow the subject: English quests are about words and stories, Mathematics quests about puzzles and codes, Science quests about discoveries, HSIE quests about explorers and places.

## Lesson code (so the parent can place any lesson at a glance)

Format: stage, week, day, lesson number, subject.

Example: S2-W03-D2-L3-ENG means Stage 2, Week 3, Day 2, third lesson of the day, English.

Subject codes: ENG, MAT, SCI, HSI, PDH, CRE (Stage 2); ENG, MAT, SCI, HIS, TEC, VIS (Stage 4, or the child's chosen subjects).

The parent view shows the code, term, week, day and slot on every lesson card, and lets the parent filter and sort by any of them. Any lesson dragged to a different week gets its code and week label updated automatically, and the parent sees a message if two lessons share a slot.

## Fixed lesson structure (60 minutes)

| Part | Oliver (Stage 2) | Mason (Stage 4) | Content |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Warm-up and recall | 5 min | 5 min | 3 to 5 quick questions from earlier lessons |
| 2. Teach | 15 min | 15 min | Definitions, explanation and worked examples built up with click-to-reveal cards; embedded video watched here |
| 3. Guided practice | 10 min | 10 min | Questions with hints; answer shown after the child tries |
| 4. Break | 3 min | optional | Stand up, drink, stretch |
| 5. Independent task | 15 min | 20 min | The main piece of work, with a success checklist and a response space |
| 6. Quiz | 7 min | 7 min | Auto-marked, 5 questions, instant feedback |
| 7. Quest complete | 5 min | 3 min | Quest finished screen, next quest teaser, daily checklist tick. No 'I can' statements |

Oliver's lesson runs as three blocks (parts 1-3, part 5, parts 6-7) with the break after block one. No games for Stage 2.

## Resources (required in every lesson)

- At least one embedded video in the teach part (Khan Academy, BBC Bitesize, Oak National Academy, YouTube, PhET or similar), with start and end times and one thing to notice.
- A 'Quest resources' panel listing every link used: videos, readings, interactive tools, worksheets. Each is opened inside the lesson page where the site allows embedding, and otherwise as a clearly labelled link with exact instructions for what to open.
- Every link is tested before release and stored with its date tested.
- Reading choices are left for the parent to set at the time.

## Lesson data fields

- lesson code, type (core or holiday), child, stage, term, week, day, slot, subject
- quest title (child), plain title (parent), story stop (which part of the week's quest it belongs to)
- one-sentence goal for the child
- NESA outcome codes and focus area (parent only)
- parent rationale and evidence collected (parent only)
- recall questions
- teaching cards (ordered: heading, text, example, optional image or emoji)
- embedded videos (provider, URL, start, end, what to notice, date tested)
- other resources (type, label, URL, instructions, date tested)
- guided practice questions (question, hint, answer)
- independent task (instructions, success checklist, response type: text, upload, drawing)
- quiz questions (type, options, correct answer, explanation)
- next quest teaser
- stuck flag ('I'm stuck' button with optional note, sends to parent)
- printable worksheet link (optional)

## Writing rules

1. Teach from the basics; never assume prior knowledge.
2. Short sentences for Oliver with a picture or emoji for each idea. Plain, clear language for Mason.
3. No instruction that needs an adult to explain it.
4. Every question has a hint and an answer to check against.
5. Revisit earlier lessons in the warm-up and quiz.
6. Quest titles on every lesson, lesson code on every lesson.
7. Every lesson has at least one embedded video and a resources panel.

## Daily checklist

Each child sees one ordered list for the day, shown as a quest map: Quest 1, Quest 2 and so on, each with a tick when finished. Oliver gets 4 quests a day, Mason 4 to 5.
