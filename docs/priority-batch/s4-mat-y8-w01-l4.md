## Teaching 1: Define rational numbers with an exact fraction

**A rational number can be written as a ratio of integers.**
The form is a/b, where a and b are integers and b is not zero. The fraction describes an exact value. One half, negative three quarters and five all qualify: five can be written as five over one.

**A denominator cannot be zero.**
Division by zero does not give an ordinary number in this definition. A fraction with zero on top and a non-zero denominator is different: zero over five equals zero and is rational.

**Different forms can name the same value.**
One half and two quarters are equal. A decimal such as 0.5 equals one half. Classify the value rather than treating each notation as a separate kind of number.

State an exact fraction as evidence when you can. A numeral’s visual length is not a classification rule.

I DO:
0.75 = 75/100 = 3/4, so it is rational.
−3 = −3/1, so negative integers are rational too.

YOU TRY:
Give a fraction for 0.2 and classify it.

CHECK:
0.2 = 2/10 = 1/5. Its exact integer-ratio representation makes it rational.

## Teaching 2: Recognise irrational values and their approximations

**An irrational number is not an exact ratio of integers.**
Its decimal representation does not terminate or repeat a fixed block forever. The square root of two and pi are examples. This lesson uses those established examples; it does not prove their irrationality.

**An approximation is a different value.**
The square root of two is about 1.414, but not exactly 1.414. The finite decimal 1.414 is rational because it equals 1414/1000. Approximating an irrational value does not make the exact value rational.

**Use the approximate sign carefully.**
The symbol ≈ means approximately equal. Use it for a rounded decimal, while = claims exact equality. Write √2 ≈ 1.414 when using that three-decimal approximation.

Avoid classifying an exact value from a few displayed digits. Identify what the symbol or problem actually specifies.

I DO:
Exact value: √2, irrational.
Approximation: 1.414, rational as a finite decimal.
Record: √2 ≈ 1.414, not √2 = 1.414.

YOU TRY:
Explain why the displayed approximation 3.14 does not make the exact value pi rational.

CHECK:
3.14 is a finite rational decimal; pi is a different exact value with a non-terminating, non-recurring decimal representation.

Notice: Exact and approximate are different labels. Keep them attached to the intended value.

**Question:** Which statement correctly distinguishes √2 and 1.414?

1. Both are exactly the same rational value
2. √2 is irrational; 1.414 is a rational approximation
3. A finite decimal can never be rational
4. The digits prove √2 terminates

Reviewer answer: √2 is irrational; 1.414 is a rational approximation

Why: The exact root is irrational, while the displayed finite decimal is an exact rational value used as an approximation.

### Help with this step

1. Find whether the question names the exact value or a display.
2. Label the approximation separately before classifying it.
3. Use equality only for exact agreement; a rounded representation needs the approximate relation.

**Squaring multiplies a value by itself.**
The notation 3² means 3 × 3 = 9, not 3 × 2. A square grid models the two equal side lengths and the resulting number of unit squares.

**The square root reverses this question.**
The notation √9 asks for the non-negative value whose square is nine. It is three. The radical symbol √ denotes the principal, non-negative square root in this lesson.

**Area and side have different units.**
If the square’s area is nine square metres, its side is three metres. The root changes a squared quantity to a length; do not label the side in square metres.

Say what is known and what is asked before calculating. This avoids confusing a side length with an area or an exponent with a multiplier.

I DO:
3² = 9, so √9 = 3.
For a square of area 9 m², side length is 3 m.

YOU TRY:
What is √16? Check by squaring your proposed answer.

CHECK:
√16 = 4 because 4 × 4 = 16. The radical denotes the non-negative root.

Notice: An exponent is not a multiplier. Square by using the value as both factors.

**Question:** What is √16?

1. 8
2. 4
3. -4
4. 32

Reviewer answer: 4

Why: The principal root is non-negative and four squared equals sixteen.

## Teaching 4: Locate non-perfect roots between anchors

**Not every positive integer is a perfect square.**
Ten lies between nine and sixteen. Because three squared is nine and four squared is sixteen, √10 lies between three and four. This gives a useful interval without guessing an exact decimal.

**Preserve the direction of the bounds.**
For non-negative values, larger squares come from larger roots. Choose the nearest square below and above the radicand, then transfer their non-negative roots to the bounds.

**An interval is not an exact answer.**
Saying three < √10 < four locates the value. It does not say the root equals three or four. A decimal estimate can refine the interval but remains approximate unless exact equality is justified.

Check the endpoints by squaring. That confirms that the original radicand sits between them and keeps the estimate anchored in the number relationship.

I DO:
9 < 10 < 16, so 3 < √10 < 4.
For √20, use 16 < 20 < 25, so 4 < √20 < 5.

YOU TRY:
Locate √30 between consecutive integer roots.

CHECK:
25 < 30 < 36, so 5 < √30 < 6.

Notice: A between statement locates an exact value; it does not replace it with an endpoint.

**Question:** Which interval contains √20?

1. Between 2 and 3
2. Between 3 and 4
3. Between 4 and 5
4. Between 5 and 6

Reviewer answer: Between 4 and 5

Why: Twenty lies between sixteen and twenty-five, whose principal roots are four and five.

### Help with this step

1. Look for the perfect squares on either side of the radicand.
2. Take their non-negative integer roots as bounds.
3. Square the bounds to verify that they enclose the original value.

## Teaching 5: Define scientific notation through place value

**Scientific notation is a × 10ⁿ.**
For a positive value, the coefficient a must be at least one and less than ten, and n is an integer exponent. The power of ten supplies the scale; the coefficient carries the significant digits.

**An exponent records repeated factors or their reciprocal.**
Ten cubed is 10 × 10 × 10 = 1,000. Ten to the zero power is one. Ten to the negative three power is 1/1,000. A negative exponent here does not make the represented positive number negative.

**Check both parts of the form.**
4.2 × 10³ is standard scientific notation. 42 × 10² has the same value but its coefficient is outside the required interval. Equivalent value and standard form are separate questions.

For this lesson we represent positive values. Zero and signed coefficients require additional conventions; do not silently extend the coefficient rule to them.

I DO:
4.2 × 10³ = 4.2 × 1,000 = 4,200.
42 × 10² also equals 4,200, but 42 is not a standard coefficient.

YOU TRY:
Is 3.5 × 10² in standard form? State the coefficient check.

CHECK:
Yes. The coefficient 3.5 is at least one and less than ten, and the exponent is an integer.

Notice: Value equality does not guarantee standard scientific-notation form.

**Question:** Which is standard scientific notation for 4,200?

1. 42 × 10²
2. 0.42 × 10⁴
3. 4,200 × 10⁰
4. 4.2 × 10³

Reviewer answer: 4.2 × 10³

Why: The value is 4,200 and the coefficient 4.2 lies in the required interval from one inclusive to ten exclusive.

### Help with this step

1. Check the coefficient’s interval before judging the whole form.
2. Evaluate the power of ten to check the represented value.
3. Require both correct value and a standard coefficient; do not choose by appearance alone.
