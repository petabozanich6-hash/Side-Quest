## Teaching 1: Define scientific notation through place value

**Scientific notation is a × 10ⁿ.**
For a positive value, the coefficient a must be at least one and less than ten, and n is an integer exponent. The power of ten supplies the scale; the coefficient carries the significant digits.

**An exponent records repeated factors or their reciprocal.**
Ten cubed is 10 × 10 × 10 = 1,000. Ten to the zero power is one. Ten to the negative three power is 1/1,000. A negative exponent here does not make the represented positive number negative.

**Check both parts of the form.**
4.2 × 10³ is standard scientific notation. 42 × 10² has the same value but its coefficient is outside the required interval. Equivalent value and standard form are separate questions.

For this lesson we represent positive values. Zero and signed coefficients require additional conventions; do not silently extend the coefficient rule to them.

I DO:
4.2 × 10³ = 4.2 × 1,000 = 4,200.
42 × 10² also equals 4,200, but 42 is not a standard coefficient.

YOU TRY:
Is 3.5 × 10² in standard form? State the coefficient check.

CHECK:
Yes. The coefficient 3.5 is at least one and less than ten, and the exponent is an integer.

## Teaching 2: Convert a large value and verify it

**Choose the coefficient first.**
For forty-five thousand, place the decimal after the first non-zero digit to get 4.5. Count how much scale is needed to recover the original value, rather than treating decimal movement as an unexplained trick.

**Restore the value with a positive power.**
4.5 × 10⁴ means 4.5 multiplied by ten thousand, giving forty-five thousand. The positive exponent expands a coefficient between one and ten into a value at least ten when the exponent is positive.

**Count place changes accurately.**
From 45,000 to 4.5 is four decimal-place changes. An exponent of three would give 4,500 instead. Multiply back to check the scale, not only the coefficient.

Preserve all required digits from the supplied exact number. This lesson is converting notation, not deciding measurement precision or rounding away meaningful digits.

I DO:
45,000 = 4.5 × 10⁴.
Check: 4.5 × 10,000 = 45,000.

YOU TRY:
Convert 72,000 and multiply back.

CHECK:
72,000 = 7.2 × 10⁴ because 7.2 × 10,000 returns the original value.

Notice: An off-by-one exponent changes the value by a factor of ten. Multiply back.

**Question:** Which represents 72,000 in standard scientific notation?

1. 7.2 × 10³
2. 7.2 × 10⁴
3. 72 × 10³
4. 0.72 × 10⁵

Reviewer answer: 7.2 × 10⁴

Why: Multiplying 7.2 by ten thousand gives 72,000 and its coefficient is standard.

### Help with this step

1. Find a coefficient with one non-zero digit before the decimal.
2. Count the scale needed to rebuild the original large number.
3. Multiply the coefficient by that power of ten and compare the exact result.

**A negative exponent scales down.**
Ten to the negative three is one thousandth. Multiplying a positive coefficient by it divides the coefficient by one thousand. The result remains positive.

**Choose a standard coefficient for the small value.**
0.0064 becomes 6.4 × 10⁻³ because 6.4 divided by one thousand is 0.0064. The leading zeroes position the value; they do not become extra significant non-zero digits.

**Keep exponent sign and number sign distinct.**
6.4 × 10⁻³ is a small positive number. The negative exponent describes the scale. It is not the same as −6.4 × 10³, which is a large negative value.

Verify through the fraction or division meaning of the power. If the answer exceeds one when the original was a small positive decimal below one, revisit the exponent direction.

I DO:
0.0064 = 6.4 × 10⁻³ = 6.4/1,000.
Check by division, not by interpreting the exponent as a negative coefficient.

YOU TRY:
Write 0.0008 in standard scientific notation.

CHECK:
8 × 10⁻⁴, because 8 divided by ten thousand is 0.0008.

Notice: The exponent sign controls scale. It does not supply the sign of the coefficient.

**Question:** Which is standard scientific notation for the positive number 0.0008?

1. 8 × 10⁴
2. −8 × 10⁴
3. 8 × 10⁻⁴
4. 0.8 × 10⁻³

Reviewer answer: 8 × 10⁻⁴

Why: Eight multiplied by one ten-thousandth is 0.0008, and eight is a standard coefficient.

## Teaching 4: Read back, compare and retain units

**Convert back before interpreting a scale.**
3.6 × 10² is 360; 3.6 × 10⁻² is 0.036. Check the exponent sign and multiplication or division before attaching a contextual conclusion.

**Compare standard positive forms carefully.**
If two standard coefficients are positive, the greater exponent gives the greater value. When the exponents match, compare the coefficients. This relies on the coefficients both satisfying the standard interval.

**Keep measurement units unchanged.**
Writing 4.5 × 10⁴ m means forty-five thousand metres. Scientific notation changes the numerical representation, not metres into kilometres. A unit conversion is a separate operation.

Show an exact value check and a unit label. If a measurement is approximate, keep that qualification; changing notation does not remove measurement uncertainty.

I DO:
3.6 × 10² m = 360 m.
3.6 × 10⁻² m = 0.036 m.
For 4.2 × 10³ and 7.1 × 10², the first is greater; conversion confirms 4,200 > 710.

YOU TRY:
Compare 2.4 × 10³ with 3.1 × 10³.

CHECK:
The exponents match, so compare 2.4 and 3.1. The second value is greater.

Notice: Notation conversion and unit conversion are different tasks.

**Question:** What is 3.6 × 10² metres in ordinary notation?

1. 360 metres
2. 0.036 metres
3. 360 kilometres
4. 36 metres

Reviewer answer: 360 metres

Why: The power scales 3.6 by one hundred, giving 360; the supplied unit remains metres.

### Help with this step

1. Read the exponent sign and retain the stated unit.
2. Convert one value back before making the contextual claim.
3. Compare standard exponents first, then coefficients when exponents match; verify by ordinary values if needed.
