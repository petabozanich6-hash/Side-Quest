## Teaching 1: Connect square roots with squaring

**Squaring multiplies a value by itself.**
The notation 3² means 3 × 3 = 9, not 3 × 2. A square grid models the two equal side lengths and the resulting number of unit squares.

**The square root reverses this question.**
The notation √9 asks for the non-negative value whose square is nine. It is three. The radical symbol √ denotes the principal, non-negative square root in this lesson.

**Area and side have different units.**
If the square’s area is nine square metres, its side is three metres. The root changes a squared quantity to a length; do not label the side in square metres.

Say what is known and what is asked before calculating. This avoids confusing a side length with an area or an exponent with a multiplier.

I DO:
3² = 9, so √9 = 3.
For a square of area 9 m², side length is 3 m.

YOU TRY:
What is √16? Check by squaring your proposed answer.

CHECK:
√16 = 4 because 4 × 4 = 16. The radical denotes the non-negative root.

Diagram: A three-by-three square grid has nine unit squares. Each side measures three units, demonstrating that the principal square root of nine is three.

Side length is the square root of square area.

Notice: An exponent is not a multiplier. Square by using the value as both factors.

Why: The principal root is non-negative and four squared equals sixteen.

## Teaching 2: Use perfect squares as exact anchors

**A perfect square is an integer square.**
Zero, one, four, nine, sixteen and twenty-five are perfect squares because they come from squaring integers. Their principal square roots are exact integers.

**Build a useful anchor list.**
Know what each anchor means: five squared is twenty-five, six squared is thirty-six, seven squared is forty-nine and eight squared is sixty-four. Do not memorise an unlabelled list and lose the factor relationship.

**Check a root through multiplication.**
A proposed square root of thirty-six is six because six times six gives thirty-six. Dividing the radicand by two answers a different operation.

Include zero where relevant: zero squared is zero and its principal root is zero. The non-negative-root convention includes it.

I DO:
5² = 25 → √25 = 5.
6² = 36 → √36 = 6.

YOU TRY:
Use the anchor list to find √49, then verify it.

CHECK:
Seven times seven is forty-nine, so √49 = 7.

Notice: Halving is not square-rooting. The inverse check is multiplying the candidate by itself.

**Question:** What is √36?

1. 18
2. -6
3. 6
4. 72

Reviewer answer: 6

Why: Six multiplied by itself gives thirty-six; the principal root is six.

### Help with this step

1. Find the relevant square anchor, not half the value.
2. Match the radicand with an integer multiplied by itself.
3. Use that non-negative factor as the root and verify its square.

**Not every positive integer is a perfect square.**
Ten lies between nine and sixteen. Because three squared is nine and four squared is sixteen, √10 lies between three and four. This gives a useful interval without guessing an exact decimal.

**Preserve the direction of the bounds.**
For non-negative values, larger squares come from larger roots. Choose the nearest square below and above the radicand, then transfer their non-negative roots to the bounds.

**An interval is not an exact answer.**
Saying three < √10 < four locates the value. It does not say the root equals three or four. A decimal estimate can refine the interval but remains approximate unless exact equality is justified.

Check the endpoints by squaring. That confirms that the original radicand sits between them and keeps the estimate anchored in the number relationship.

I DO:
9 < 10 < 16, so 3 < √10 < 4.
For √20, use 16 < 20 < 25, so 4 < √20 < 5.

YOU TRY:
Locate √30 between consecutive integer roots.

CHECK:
25 < 30 < 36, so 5 < √30 < 6.

Notice: A between statement locates an exact value; it does not replace it with an endpoint.

**Question:** Which interval contains √20?

1. Between 2 and 3
2. Between 3 and 4
3. Between 4 and 5
4. Between 5 and 6

Reviewer answer: Between 4 and 5

Why: Twenty lies between sixteen and twenty-five, whose principal roots are four and five.

### Help with this step

1. Look for the perfect squares on either side of the radicand.
2. Take their non-negative integer roots as bounds.
3. Square the bounds to verify that they enclose the original value.

## Teaching 4: Distinguish a principal root from equation solutions

**The radical gives one principal root.**
√25 equals five. It does not mean both five and negative five. That is the notation convention used for the root expression.

**A squared-variable equation can have two solutions.**
For x² = 25, both x = 5 and x = −5 work because each squares to twenty-five. This is a different question from evaluating √25.

**Use context to select a meaningful solution.**
A square’s side length is non-negative, so an area calculation uses the principal root for the length. The equation’s negative solution is mathematically valid but not a negative physical side length.

Read the prompt carefully: evaluate a radical, solve an equation or find a physical length. Then check candidate values in the appropriate original relation.

I DO:
Expression: √25 = 5.
Equation: x² = 25 → x = 5 or x = −5.
Side of a square with area 25 m²: 5 m.

YOU TRY:
Explain why −4 is a solution of x² = 16 but not the value of √16.

CHECK:
(−4) × (−4) = 16, but √ denotes the principal non-negative root, which is four.

Notice: Do not write two values for a principal radical. Keep expression and equation questions separate.

**Question:** Which statement respects the principal-root convention?

1. √25 = −5 only
2. √25 means both signs at once
3. A square can have a negative side length
4. √25 = 5; x² = 25 has solutions 5 and −5

Reviewer answer: √25 = 5; x² = 25 has solutions 5 and −5

Why: The radical denotes the non-negative root; the squared equation accepts either sign because both square to twenty-five.

### Help with this step

1. Look at whether the prompt is a radical or an equation.
2. Check each proposed solution by squaring, then apply the notation convention.
3. For a length, choose the non-negative result and give length units.
