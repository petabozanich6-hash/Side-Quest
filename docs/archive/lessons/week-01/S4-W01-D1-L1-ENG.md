**Quest title:** Decode the Explorer's Journal
**Plain title:** Reading strategies
**Goal for Mason:** Use a set of reading strategies to work out what a difficult text means.

**Parent only:** Outcome EN4-RVL-01 (check against NESA; reading, viewing and listening to texts, personal, creative and critical reading strategies). Rationale: starts the term with explicit strategies that later lessons rely on. Evidence: annotated text upload, quiz score.

## Part 1: Recall (5 min)
1. What is the difference between fiction and non-fiction? Answer: fiction is invented, non-fiction is based on facts.
2. What is a verb? Answer: a word for an action, state or happening.
3. What does 'infer' mean? Hint: reading between the lines. Answer: to work out something that is not stated directly.

## Part 2: Teach (15 min)
Card 1, Why strategies matter. Good readers do not just read words, they use strategies, which are deliberate techniques, to build meaning when a text is complex.
Card 2, Five strategies. Predict (guess what will happen from clues). Question (ask what, why, how while reading). Infer (combine clues with what you know). Visualise (picture the scene). Summarise (state the main points in your own words).
Card 3, Context clues. When a word is unknown, check the words around it. Example: 'The expedition was perilous; one wrong step could mean a fall into the gorge.' The second half of the sentence shows that 'perilous' means dangerous.
Card 4, Worked example. Read: 'The journal's pages were stiff with salt, and the last entry ended mid-sentence.' Infer: the journal has been in seawater and the writer was interrupted or in trouble. Question: What happened to the writer? Predict: the next entry may be missing.

## Part 3: Guided practice (10 min)
Text: 'By the third day, the water barrel was nearly empty. Marek stopped talking and stared at the horizon.'
Q1 Infer how Marek feels. Hint: look at what he stops doing. Answer: worried or anxious.
Q2 Which words support your inference? Hint: find the evidence. Answer: 'nearly empty' and 'stopped talking and stared'.
Q3 Write one question you would ask the writer. Answer: any question about what happens next.

## Part 4: Break (optional, 3 min)

## Part 5: Independent task (20 min)
Read the journal extract below and complete a strategy table (one row per strategy: Predict, Question, Infer, Visualise, Summarise). Each row needs a quote from the text as evidence.

'Day 9. The storm took the mast. We are drifting, though to where I cannot say. Pia insists the stars are moving the wrong way; I told her it was fatigue, but I have watched them for an hour and I am no longer certain. We have rationed the biscuits to four each. Tomorrow, if the swell drops, I will try to read the charts again.'

Success checklist: five strategies each used once; each row has a quote; my summary is under 30 words.
Response type: text table, or upload a photo of handwritten work.

Parent option: set a longer reading for later in the week.

## Part 6: Quiz (7 min)
1. An inference is (a) a fact stated in the text (b) a conclusion drawn from clues plus what you know (c) a spelling rule. Answer b.
2. Which strategy means picturing a scene? Answer: visualise.
3. In 'perilous; one wrong step could mean a fall', perilous probably means (a) dangerous (b) cheerful (c) slow. Answer a.
4. Evidence for an inference should come from (a) the text (b) a guess (c) another book. Answer a.
5. Which is a good summary of the extract? Options include one accurate under-30-word summary. Answer: the one that names the storm, uncertainty about direction and the ration.
